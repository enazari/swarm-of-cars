Rule-based vs rule-based + diffusion

Every time a car appears at your mouse, two cars appear on the same spot with the same goal, one in each of two identical worlds that run at once and never see each other: R is steered by hand-written rules, D by a small diffusion network (the World button switches between only R, only D, the two worlds on top of each other, or one shared world in which R and D cars are the same traffic and collide; every switch starts over with an empty stage). Both get the same GPS-style route around the cards (a classic shortest-path search); that route is all the rules use. The network also sees the map and the nearby cars, and every 0.25 s denoises a 2 s plan from random noise (the lines are those plans: bold = the part the car drives). Compare how they handle cards and traffic: orange rings are impacts, a red ring means a car has not moved for 10 s. You can drag the cards (they shove cars) and grab and throw cars.

Drag the cards to change the map: every car re-reads it on the next step. The policy was trained on layouts with gaps of at least 45 px between cards,
    so very tight gaps are outside what it has seen. The strict rule-based driver follows the route and brakes for a car straight ahead, nothing more. Each card has an inverted-U tunnel entrance cut into its bottom edge. Grab a car with the mouse and let go to throw it with the speed of the mouse; drag a card into cars and it shoves them with its momentum. Cars never overlap a block: they can touch it and are pushed back. Cars drive in from the front; inside the U they only brush the sides without bouncing, and once at the middle they go on through the back, left or right wall and out of sight. Cars have real collision physics: a hit car slides on in the direction of the push and bounces off cards by speed and angle, and nothing steps in to stop a crash.
